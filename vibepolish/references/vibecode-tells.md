# Vibe-code tells

Concrete search candidates for unfinished or template-like presentation. These help find overlooked details; they do not establish authorship, a defect, or its severity on their own.

## How to use this list

- Use targeted source/asset searches to find candidates, then inspect the context. Confirm user-visible effects in the rendered app when available; otherwise label runtime verification as unavailable.
- Classify a candidate as a confirmed defect only when evidence shows a violated requirement, unintended leftover, broken behavior, or meaningful usability problem. Native controls, library defaults, and familiar visual patterns are not inherently defects.
- Search results are not findings. Exclude tests, examples, documentation, and intentional demos from claims about production output unless they actually surface there.
- Respect intentional brand decisions. Style recommendations need a product-specific rationale; punctuation and layout conventions do not prove AI generation.
- Group several evidenced inconsistencies on a key page when their combined effect matters. Explain that effect and avoid duplicate findings or severity inflation.
- For focused requests, inspect only relevant sections and directly affected dependencies. Use this checklist alongside product-risk checks rather than always before them.

## 1. Framework and template defaults

- Page titles such as "Create Next App", "Vite + React", "Vite + Vue + TS",
  "React App", "SvelteKit app", "Astro", "Nuxt", "My App", "Home", "Untitled",
  or the repository name, when they conflict with the intended product/page identity. A title such as "Home" may be intentional; assess its usefulness in context.
- Default meta descriptions ("Generated by create next app", "Web site created
  using create-react-app") or none at all on public pages.
- Default favicons and logos (framework, hosting, or template icons such as
  `vite.svg`, `next.svg`, `vercel.svg`, `react.svg`) still shipped or referenced.
- Starter-page remnants: "Get started by editing…", "Edit src/App.tsx and save to
  reload", counter demos, "Deploy now" / "Read our docs" buttons, template
  footer links.
- Template README, manifest (`"name": "React App"`), `package.json` name or
  version left at defaults where they surface to users or metadata.
- UI-kit or theme demo content: sample dashboards, fake charts, placeholder
  avatars, example company names.

## 2. Leftovers and placeholders

- Lorem ipsum, "Your text here", "Coming soon" without a date or context,
  "TODO", "FIXME", "TBD", "xxx" in visible UI or metadata.
- Test data visible in production: "John Doe", "Jane Smith", "test@test.com",
  "example.com" links, "Acme Inc.", "123 Main Street", "+1 555-…".
- Placeholder images (`placeholder.com`, `picsum`, `unsplash` random URLs, gray
  boxes) and broken image links.
- `href="#"`, `href=""`, `javascript:void(0)`, empty `onClick` handlers, social
  icons linking to platform homepages instead of real profiles.
- Footer dates inconsistent with the intended publication/update information; legal or contact links pointing nowhere. A past copyright year alone is not evidence of a defect; do not automatically replace it with the current year.
- Unintended debug output, `debugger`, internal feature flags, or raw payloads exposed to users. Logs and visible IDs may be intentional diagnostics or useful product information (for example, an order number); verify purpose before proposing removal.
- Commented-out sections still rendering stray wrappers or spacing.

## 3. Unfinished edges

- Generic error texts ("Something went wrong", "An error occurred", "Error: 500")
  with no next step; raw technical errors, `undefined`, `null`, `NaN`,
  `[object Object]`, or `Invalid Date` in the UI.
- Loading indicators that conceal important state, block useful interaction, or never resolve. Reusing one spinner is acceptable when it communicates the state adequately.
- Lists, tables, and dashboards with no empty state (blank area or a bare "No data").
- Missing-page experiences that leave users stranded or conflict with the product requirements. A framework-provided 404 is acceptable when it gives appropriate status and recovery.
- Dead-end pages without navigation back.
- Evaluate keyboard focus under the accessibility checks in [Product quality](product-quality.md). Assess hover, cursor, and other visual feedback according to the interaction; their absence alone is not a defect. Missing pending/disabled behavior matters when it permits unintended repeated actions or hides progress.
- Validation behavior that is conflicting, inaccessible, unclear, or inconsistent with required interactions. Native browser validation is acceptable and need not be replaced for visual uniformity alone.

## 4. Copy and content tells

- Generic AI marketing phrases: "Unlock the power of", "Revolutionize your",
  "Seamless", "Effortlessly", "Elevate your", "Supercharge", "Harness",
  "Next-level", "Game-changer", "Your all-in-one solution", "In today's
  fast-paced world", "Take your X to the next level". Also their equivalents in the
  app's language.
- Headlines that could describe any product; feature texts without concrete
  nouns, numbers, or examples from this product.
- Claims the product cannot support: features described but not implemented,
  "Trusted by thousands" without evidence, invented metrics, unverifiable
  testimonials, partner logos without a known relationship. Treat these as
  content-credibility findings and ask for evidence rather than asserting
  fabrication.
- Mixed UI languages (e.g. English buttons, dates, or validation errors in a
  German app), and mixed forms of address (formal and informal).
- Wrong locale formats: dates, numbers, decimal separators, currency symbols and
  position, time format, first day of week.
- Inconsistent capitalization of buttons and headings; inconsistent terms for the
  same thing (e.g. "Project" / "Workspace" / "Space").
- Emoji-heavy or exclamation-heavy copy in a product whose audience expects a
  professional tone; typos.
- Repetitive sentence structures or stock phrasing that obscure the product message. Evaluate clarity and tone; em dashes, triplets, and individual words are not authorship tests or defects by themselves.

## 5. Visual patterns

Recommendations unless they cause a concrete problem. Explain criticism through
readability, clarity, accessibility, consistency, brand fit, or repetition.

- Default purple-to-blue or indigo gradients, gradient text on many headings,
  glow effects and glassmorphism without purpose.
- Every section built the same way: centered hero, three identical feature cards
  with an icon or emoji on top, a stats row, a testimonial grid, a CTA banner.
- Emojis used as interface icons; several icon sets mixed; icons of different
  sizes or stroke widths.
- Untouched component-library defaults (default shadcn/ui, MUI, Bootstrap,
  Tailwind UI look) combined with a few custom one-off components that do not match.
- Arbitrary spacing values and border radii that drift between pages; several
  near-identical shades of the same color; hardcoded colors next to design tokens.
- Everything animated on scroll; animations that delay access to content.
- Incomplete dark mode on surfaces where the product promises/supports it, or disruptive theme flashes. Email-client theme handling is a separate context; do not assume every email must match the app theme.
- Stretched or badly cropped images; inconsistent image aspect ratios in grids.
- Layout visibly designed for one screen width (text lines too long on wide
  screens, cramped cards on small ones).

## 6. Quick detection hints

Adapt paths and patterns to the discovered stack. Exclude `node_modules`, build
output, and lockfiles. Never print secret values if a search hits configuration.

- Defaults: search for `Create Next App`, `Vite +`, `React App`, `create-react-app`,
  `Get started by editing`, `vite.svg`, `next.svg`, `vercel.svg`.
- Placeholders: search for `lorem`, `ipsum`, `TODO`, `FIXME`, `John Doe`,
  `test@`, `example.com`, `Acme`, `placeholder`, `picsum`.
- Dead controls: search for `href="#"`, `href=""`, and potentially inert handlers across template syntaxes: `javascript:void`, `void 0`, empty functions, `() => null`, and `noop`. Verify the control’s intended behavior; an intentional no-op is not automatically a defect.
- Debug output: search for `console.log`, `console.debug`, `debugger`,
  `JSON.stringify(` inside rendered markup.
- Copy: search the app's visible strings for the phrases in section 4.
- Dates: inspect hardcoded years in context; do not infer staleness from the year alone.
- Rendered check: open key pages and read the tab title, favicon, and social preview
  exactly as a visitor would see them.
